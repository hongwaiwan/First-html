Electron: Particle and Wave

1. Particle Properties

  • Point-like: No known internal structure or volume.
  • Mass: ~0.511 MeV, entirely from the Higgs field.
  • Charge: -1 e, concentrated at a point.
  • Spin: 1/2 (intrinsic quantum angular momentum).
  • Interactions: Electromagnetic, weak force, and gravity.

Particle-like behavior is observed in collisions, deflection by fields, and energy transfer in experiments like the photoelectric effect.

2. Wave Properties

  • Described by a wavefunction ψ(r,t), giving the probability of finding the electron at a position.
  • Exhibits interference and diffraction, as in the double-slit experiment and electron diffraction in crystals.
  • Wave-particle duality: behaves like a localized particle during measurements and like a delocalized wave in interference patterns.

3. Energy and Motion

  • Even as a point particle, electrons have kinetic energy and momentum.
  • In atoms, electrons occupy quantized orbitals, which are wave patterns of probability.
  • Electron energy comes from both rest mass (Higgs) and motion/interactions (kinetic + potential energy).

4. Particle and Wave Coexistence

  • Point-like nature does not conflict with wave behavior.
  • The wave is a mathematical description of probability, not a literal “spread-out particle.”
  • Experiments detect localized impacts, but statistical patterns reveal interference.

Summary

  • Fundamental, point-like particle with mass, charge, and spin.
  • Mass comes from the Higgs field; no internal structure.
  • Wave-like quantum behavior described by a wavefunction.
  • Dual nature: particle in measurements, wave in interference.
  • Energy arises from rest mass and interactions with fields.
